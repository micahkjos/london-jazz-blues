The Scene

Who gets to call it jazz?

London's jazz story is usually told through a handful of rooms. There's Ronnie Scott's, opened in a Soho basement in 1959, where American stars were billed as the real thing and local players as their support. There's the 606 in Chelsea, open since 1976, which describes itself as "Jazz :: Latin :: Soul & More" because one word has never been enough.

The words on the posters have always shaped who is seen. The British blues boom of the 1960s turned Black American music into a white British rock export, and the word "authentic" did a lot of work on the posters. More recently, the press label "the new London jazz scene" has grouped together musicians from the city's Caribbean and West African communities. Many of them came through Tomorrow's Warriors, the education charity that has been training young players since 1991. Ezra Collective's 2023 Mercury Prize, the first ever won by a jazz act, changed the headlines more than it changed who books the rooms.
